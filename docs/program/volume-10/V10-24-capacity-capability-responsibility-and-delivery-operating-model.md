## 1. Purpose

This chapter defines the required capability and capacity demand for the
affiliation vertical and the delivery-operating model. It is a planning model. It
authorizes no hiring, no staff assignment, and no consultant engagement.

## 2. Capability domains

Capability demand (register REG-1001, kind `CAPABILITY_DEMAND`) spans executive
sponsorship, program and product leadership, domain ownership, architecture,
application engineering, data engineering, infrastructure and cloud, security,
privacy and records, quality engineering, accessibility, bilingual content,
migration, finance and procurement, operations and support, change and adoption,
and independent assurance.

## 3. Per-capability attributes

For every capability the model records demand by wave, required competence,
decision rights, independence requirement, minimum viable capacity, preferred
capacity, current evidence of availability, single-person dependency,
external-service dependency, capacity risk, mitigation option, and commitment
status.

## 4. Capacity distinctions

- **A named role is not an assigned person and is not a confirmed capacity.**
- An available person is not a confirmed capacity.
- A capacity model is not a hiring authorization and is not a consultant
  engagement.

## 5. Solo-delivery concentration risk

The current **solo-delivery** concentration is recorded as a material planning
risk and is not normalized away. Several capability domains presently depend on a
single person; this single-person dependency is carried explicitly in the capacity
records and in the risk register (V10-30) so that it remains visible to executive
decision-making rather than hidden inside an optimistic capacity assumption.
