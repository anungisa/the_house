## 1. Purpose

This chapter defines the planning hierarchy and the work-breakdown structure by
which the club-affiliation delivery outcome is decomposed into governed,
traceable work. The hierarchy is a planning device; it authorizes no execution.

## 2. Planning hierarchy

The planning hierarchy is controlled from program outcome down to acceptance
criterion:

1. Program outcome — the governed capability to be delivered.
2. Delivery objective — a bounded objective contributing to the outcome.
3. Capability — a competence required to deliver.
4. Workstream — a coordinating grouping of work.
5. Work package — a bounded, ownable unit of planned work.
6. Deliverable — a documentary or, later, constructed output.
7. Evidence obligation — the evidence a later gate will require.
8. Acceptance criterion — the basis on which a release is accepted.

The delivery model register (REG-1001) records outcomes, capabilities,
workstreams, work packages, deliverables, and dependencies. Every record is a
plan; none authorizes implementation.

## 3. Work-breakdown structure

The work-breakdown structure decomposes the outcome into work packages under
workstreams. Each work package is traceable upward to an objective and outcome
and downward to deliverables and evidence obligations. The work-breakdown
structure is complete when every material inherited obligation has either a
work-package destination or a governed disposition recorded in the planning
backlog.

## 4. Work-package obligations

Every work package must identify, at planning level:

- an objective and a bounded scope, with explicitly excluded scope;
- the source requirements it satisfies;
- a responsible role;
- the capabilities it requires;
- its dependencies;
- its deliverables;
- a definition of ready and a definition of done;
- its evidence obligations;
- its security and privacy obligations;
- its accessibility and bilingual obligations;
- the future gate that will authorize its execution.

These obligations are enforced by the delivery-model schema and the structural
control. A work-package plan is not a construction authorization; construction is
authorized only at a later implementation-authorization gate.
