## Purpose

This chapter defines the continuity, backup, restore, and recovery obligations for
the governed services. It defines these obligations in documentary form only; it
takes no backup, performs no restore, and recovers nothing.

## Continuity scenarios

Continuity scenarios are recorded in REG-1102 as continuity-scenario records. Each
scenario defines the work that must be preserved, the actions that are prohibited
during the scenario, the controlled degraded mode, the fallback procedure, and the
recovery procedure. Continuity is the discipline of preserving governed work and
prohibiting destructive action during disruption; it is distinct from backup, from
restore, and from recovery.

## Backup distinctions

Backup obligations are recorded as backup-requirement records. The controlling
distinction is:

> Backup completed is not backup verified is not backup restorable.

A backup that has been captured is merely complete. A backup whose integrity and
completeness have been independently checked is verified. A backup that has been
reproduced into a target environment through a governed restore is restorable.
These are three separate states, and a backup may not be treated as restorable
merely because it is complete.

## Restore, recovery, and reconciliation distinctions

Restore, recovery, and reconciliation obligations are recorded as
restore-requirement, recovery-requirement, and reconciliation obligations. The
controlling distinction is:

> Restoration is not recovery and is not reconciliation.

Restoration reproduces a verified dataset in a target environment. Recovery returns
the business service to an accepted operating state. Reconciliation confirms that
the restored and recovered state is consistent with governed state and with the
source. Reproducing data is not recovering a service, and recovering a service is
not confirming that its state reconciles. Each is a distinct obligation with its
own evidence.

## Non-execution

None of the continuity, backup, restore, recovery, or reconciliation obligations
defined here is exercised by this chapter. Each is a governance obligation to be
satisfied under future authorization, with its own evidence, at the appropriate
operating state.
