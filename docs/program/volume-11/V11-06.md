## Purpose

This chapter defines how legacy records would be migrated onto the governed
services: the migration stages, the preservation obligations, and the distinctions
that keep migration honest. It defines these obligations in documentary form only;
it migrates nothing, and it authorizes no cutover and no source retirement.

## Migration stages

Migration stages are recorded in REG-1102 as migration-stage records. Each stage
preserves source authority, preserves provenance, preserves uncertainty, treats
identity candidates as candidates, quarantines records that fail validation, and
bounds rehearsal, cutover, acceptance, rollback, and source retirement. Throughout
migration the source system remains authoritative until an explicit, evidenced
authorization says otherwise.

## Mapping versus identity

The first controlling distinction is:

> Mapping complete is not identity resolved.

Having mapped every source field to a target field does not mean that the identity
of the underlying entity has been resolved. Identity candidates remain candidates
until they are resolved with evidence; a complete mapping never silently asserts a
resolved identity.

## Rehearsal versus authorization

The second controlling distinction is:

> Rehearsal is not migration authorization.

Rehearsing the migration — proving the approach against real data without mutating
any system of record — demonstrates feasibility. It does not authorize migration.
A successful rehearsal is a precondition for authorization, never the authorization
itself.

## Cutover, acceptance, and retirement

The third controlling distinction is:

> Cutover is not acceptance and is not source retirement.

Cutting over to the new service is not the same as the business accepting it, and
neither is the same as retiring the source. Cutover, business acceptance, and
source retirement are three separate, separately evidenced, separately authorized
events. Outstanding uncertainty, unresolved identity candidates, or outstanding
quarantine block cutover authorization; a defined rollback must exist before
cutover authorization; and source retirement is a distinct, later authorization.

## Reconciliation

Reconciliation obligations are recorded as reconciliation records. Reconciliation
compares migrated records against source and governed state, reporting differences
with retained evidence and never silently correcting them. Reconciliation is a
distinct obligation from mapping, from cutover, and from acceptance.
