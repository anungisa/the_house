## Purpose

This chapter provides the integrated coverage assessment, operational-evidence plan,
and backlog assessment for the affiliation operating model. It defines obligations
only and authorizes no operation, acceptance, or release.

## Integrated coverage

The affiliation operating model is assessed for coverage across services and
ownership, operating states and stabilization, support and escalation, observability
and incident response, continuity and recovery, migration and retirement, data quality
and reconciliation, training and adoption, and provider operations. Every material
affiliation service has a recorded owner or an explicitly recorded governed ownership
gap, and all twelve ownership dimensions are represented. The deterministic
affiliation-operating-model analysis reports coverage gaps as blocking findings.

## Operational-evidence plan

Every operational-evidence requirement in REG-1102 binds environment, configuration,
version, actor identity, organization, jurisdiction, data reference, provider state,
and time. Operational evidence destinations are defined for Volume 12 assurance:
operational-readiness evidence, migration evidence, continuity and recovery evidence,
training and adoption evidence, provider assurance evidence, and independent
operational-acceptance evidence each carry a recorded Volume 12 destination.

House P0 findings have operational-proof and release-evidence destinations, so that
each House P0 operational-assurance obligation is bound to a future proof and a future
release-evidence artifact rather than treated as satisfied.

## Backlog assessment

Every unresolved assumption, risk, issue, migration-backlog item, adoption-backlog
item, and assurance gap in REG-1104 records an owner, required evidence, and a valid
future blocking gate. No unresolved item points to a completed gate; every unresolved
item points forward to V12-G1 or a later gate.

## Boundary

This chapter records an assessment and an evidence plan. It does not accept, release,
operate, or authorize any affiliation service, and no evidence in this chapter is
treated as operational proof.
