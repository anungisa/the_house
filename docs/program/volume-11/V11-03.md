## Purpose

This chapter defines how the governed services would be supported: the classes of
support work, the distinctions between them, and the boundary between support
access and governed authority. It defines obligations only and performs no support.

## Support classes and their distinctions

Support work is classified in REG-1101 as support-class records. The classes and
their distinctions are held explicit so that they are never collapsed into one
another:

- A **request** is a governed ask for assistance or a standard change that does
  not itself mutate governed state.
- An **incident** is an unplanned interruption or reduction in service quality.
- A **problem** is the underlying cause of one or more incidents, and is distinct
  from a **workaround**, which relieves a symptom without removing the cause, and
  from a **defect**, which is a specific fault to be corrected.
- The closing of a support item is an **obligation closure**: an item is closed
  only when its governed obligation has been discharged with evidence, never
  merely because activity has stopped.

A request is not an incident. An incident is not a problem. A workaround is not a
defect. A closed activity is not an obligation closure.

## Support access versus authority

The controlling distinction of this chapter is:

> Support access is distinct from mutation and from institutional authority.

Being granted support access to assist a user or diagnose an incident never, by
itself, confers the authority to mutate governed state, and never confers
institutional authority over affiliation outcomes. Any change to governed state
flows only through the governed transitions defined in the inherited kernel;
support access is read-and-assist within governed policy. Institutional authority
over affiliation outcomes remains entirely separate from support access.

## Escalation

Every support class carries an escalation route. Support escalates to the
operations owner and, where institutional authority is implicated, to the
executive sponsor. Escalation never silently transfers authority; it routes a
matter to the party who holds the relevant governed authority.
