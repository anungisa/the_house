## Purpose

This chapter defines how external providers would be governed across their
operational life: their responsibilities during operation, disruption, and exit,
and the boundary between provider certification and end-to-end assurance. It
defines these obligations in documentary form only; it engages no provider and
integrates nothing.

## Providers

Providers are recorded in REG-1101 as provider records. Each provider names its
role, its certification boundary, and its end-to-end assurance boundary. Providers
supply platform, storage, messaging, and identity, but they never supply the
governed affiliation outcome.

## Provider operational obligations

The provider operations model defines obligations across the whole provider
relationship:

- **incident** — how a provider incident is raised, handled, and evidenced;
- **continuity** — how service continuity is preserved when a provider is
  disrupted;
- **return** — how data and control are returned from a provider on request;
- **deletion** — how a provider deletes data when required;
- **residual** — how residual data and obligations are identified and discharged
  after a provider relationship ends;
- **substitution** — how one provider would be substituted for another;
- **reconciliation** — how state is reconciled after a provider incident,
  substitution, or exit;
- **exit** — how a provider relationship is wound down under governance.

Each obligation is defined and evidenced separately, and none of them is exercised
in this package.

## Certification versus assurance

The controlling distinction of this chapter is:

> Provider certification is not end-to-end assurance.

A provider's certifications attest to the provider's own platform. They do not
attest to the governed affiliation service that runs upon it. End-to-end assurance
of the affiliated service — that the whole service produces correct, governed
outcomes — remains an independent obligation and is never satisfied by accepting a
provider's certification in its place. This distinction is recorded as decision
ADR-V11-003.
