## Purpose

This chapter defines the operating states a governed service moves through and the
distinctions between them. It defines the states as governance obligations; it
places no service into any state and operates nothing.

## The operating states

The operating states are recorded in REG-1101 as operating-state records. In
order, they are: defined, implemented, verified, release candidate, operationally
prepared, operationally accepted, deployed, stabilizing, operating, degraded,
suspended, recovering, and retired. Each state has an entry condition, an exit
condition, and a distinction from adjacent states.

## Distinctions that must not be collapsed

Several transitions are routinely conflated and are therefore held explicitly
distinct:

> Operationally prepared is distinct from operationally accepted.

Preparing a service — assembling its operational readiness evidence — is not the
same as an independent authority accepting it for operation.

> Deployed is distinct from stabilizing.

Placing a service into an environment is not the same as it having stabilized under
real conditions.

> Stabilizing is distinct from operating.

A stabilizing service is still settling; a steadily operating service has reached
an accepted steady state.

## Controlled non-steady states

Beyond steady operation, four non-steady states are defined and held under
control rather than treated as uncontrolled failure:

- A **degraded** service is operating in a controlled reduced mode with defined
  limits and notifications.
- A **suspended** service has been deliberately and reversibly stopped under a
  governed decision.
- A **recovering** service is executing a governed recovery procedure back toward
  an accepted operating state.
- A **retired** service has reached a controlled terminal state with its evidence
  retained.

Each of these states is a controlled, governed condition — never an ungoverned
accident. Entry to and exit from each is defined in REG-1101, and none of these
states is entered by this documentary definition.
