## V7-32.1 Purpose

This section is normative.

This chapter opens Volume 7 Package 4. Package 4 defines how the club-affiliation experience corpus produced by Packages 1, 2, and 3 is to be validated and handed off toward implementation planning. It does not perform validation, does not conduct research, does not approve content, and does not authorize implementation. This chapter establishes the validation doctrine: the families of validation that the experience requires, the evidence each family may and may not rely on, and the boundaries that keep one kind of evidence from being mistaken for another. Package 4 defines the protocols; the execution of those protocols is authorized only by a future gate.

## V7-32.2 Inherited baseline and additive discipline

This section is normative.

Package 4 inherits the corrected Volume 6 release baseline central-registration-volume-6-v1.0.1, the frozen Package 1 experience foundation, the frozen Package 2 interaction model as corrected by the Package 2 governance amendment, and the frozen Package 3 visual, component, content, and reference-prototype definition as clarified by the Package 3 governance amendment. Package 4 supersedes nothing. It adds validation and handoff definition on top of the frozen corpus. No Package 4 record modifies a frozen chapter, and no Package 4 record weakens an inherited audit, evidence, accessibility, bilingual, privacy, or non-implementation control.

## V7-32.3 The validation families and their separation

This section is normative.

The experience requires distinct families of validation, and no family may stand in for another. Design completeness confirms that a specification names every required state, source, and constraint; it is not expert review. Expert review is qualified judgement that a specification is coherent and defensible; it is not prototype evaluation. Prototype evaluation examines a reference candidate for internal consistency; it is not usability evaluation. Usability evaluation observes representative participants attempting governed tasks; it is not accessibility verification. Accessibility verification confirms conformance obligations against assistive technology; it is not bilingual validation. Bilingual validation confirms semantic equivalence of English and French; it is not operational validation. Operational validation confirms that support, staff, and service processes can sustain the experience; it is not stakeholder acceptance. Stakeholder acceptance is a governed decision that the defined experience is acceptable to proceed; it is not production approval. Production approval is a separate future authorization that no Package 4 artifact grants. Each family is recorded in register REG-702 as a validation family with its own objective, scope, permitted evidence, prohibited inference, qualification requirement, environment, defect treatment, retest rule, acceptance authority, and future gate authority.

## V7-32.4 Evidence boundaries

This section is normative.

Each validation family names the evidence it may rely on and the inference it must never draw. Permitted evidence is bounded to what the family can actually observe. Prohibited inference names the conclusion that the evidence cannot support: design completeness never implies usability; a passed automated scan never implies accessibility conformance; a machine or literal translation never implies bilingual semantic equivalence; a working build never implies acceptance; and a favourable measurement never implies institutional truth. Evidence gathered under one family is not transferable to another family as proof. Every validation family fails closed: where the qualifying evidence for a claim is absent, the claim is not established, and the corpus continues to record the underlying obligation as not proven.

## V7-32.5 Qualification, environment, and defect treatment

This section is normative.

Every validation family names who is qualified to conduct it, the environment in which it must be conducted, how defects are recorded and dispositioned, and the conditions under which a retest is required. Qualification prevents unqualified attestation from being treated as validation. Environment prevents evidence from an unrepresentative setting from being treated as representative. Defect treatment ensures that findings are recorded as governed backlog items rather than silently resolved, and retest rules ensure that a corrected artifact is re-evaluated rather than assumed fixed. No defect is closed on the basis of a design-file change alone.

## V7-32.6 Acceptance authority and future gates

This section is normative.

Each validation family names the acceptance authority empowered to accept its result and the future gate at which that acceptance may occur. Acceptance authority for a validation family is never the same authority that authored the artifact under validation, preserving separation between authorship and acceptance. No Package 4 record accepts any validation result; Package 4 only defines who may accept it and when. The future gate authority named on each family is a forward gate; it is never a gate that has already been dispositioned.

## V7-32.7 Explicit non-authorizations

This section is normative.

This chapter authorizes no implementation. It performs no user research, conducts no usability, accessibility, or bilingual evaluation, and makes no conformance claim. It creates no production user interface, production content, validated translation, coded interface, design-system implementation, executable workflow, or interface or integration contract, and no procurement, sequencing, staffing, cost, pilot, rollout, or master development plan. Every controlled record remains in a not-implemented-or-not-proven posture.
