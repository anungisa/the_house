## V7-34.1 Purpose

This section is normative.

This chapter defines the usability research plan for the club-affiliation experience and the governance under which participants are recruited, consented, observed, and protected. It defines the plan only. No usability research is performed by Package 4, and no usability claim is established.

## V7-34.2 Objective and scope

This section is normative.

The objective of usability research is to observe whether representative participants can complete the governed affiliation tasks defined in chapter V7-33 with understanding, efficiency, and confidence, and to surface where the experience misleads, obstructs, or burdens them. Its scope is the governed task protocols and their path classes. Usability research is bounded: it does not evaluate accessibility conformance, bilingual equivalence, visual finality, or operational readiness, each of which is a separate family under chapter V7-32.

## V7-34.3 Participant governance and consent

This section is normative.

Participants are recruited to represent the governed actors of the affiliation experience, not for convenience. Every participant provides informed consent that names what will be observed, how observations are recorded, how long records are retained, and how a participant may withdraw. Participant recruitment and consent honour the inherited privacy postures: no research activity expands access to personal, financial, or restricted evidence beyond what the governed experience already permits, and no sensitive information is copied into research artifacts. Consent is explicit and revocable, and the plan fails closed where consent is absent.

## V7-34.4 Method, environment, and qualification

This section is normative.

The plan names the research method, the environment in which sessions are conducted, and the qualification required of those conducting and interpreting them. The environment must be representative of the governed contexts, including low-bandwidth and interrupted-service conditions. Qualification prevents casual observation from being recorded as usability evidence. The method names how tasks are presented, how assistance is bounded, and how observations are captured without leading the participant.

## V7-34.5 Evidence, defect treatment, and prohibited inference

This section is normative.

Usability evidence is the observed behaviour and reported experience of qualified sessions. Findings are recorded as governed backlog items with owners and forward gates; they are not silently resolved. The plan names its prohibited inferences: a small or unrepresentative sample proves nothing about the population; the absence of an observed failure is not proof of success; and favourable sessions do not establish accessibility, bilingual equivalence, or production readiness. A corrected artifact is re-evaluated under a named retest rule rather than assumed improved.

## V7-34.6 Acceptance authority and future gate

This section is normative.

The plan names the acceptance authority empowered to accept usability results and the forward gate at which acceptance may occur. That authority is distinct from the authors of the experience under study. Package 4 defines who may accept usability evidence and when; it accepts none.

## V7-34.7 Explicit non-authorizations

This section is normative.

This chapter authorizes no implementation. It recruits no participants, runs no sessions, and establishes no usability claim. It creates no production user interface, production content, validated translation, coded interface, design-system implementation, executable workflow, or interface or integration contract, and no procurement, sequencing, staffing, cost, pilot, rollout, or master development plan. Every controlled record remains in a not-implemented-or-not-proven posture.
