## V7-37.1 Purpose

This section is normative.

This chapter defines the governance model for the content of the club-affiliation experience: how content is authored, approved, versioned, retired, and localized. It defines the model only. It approves no content and creates no production content.

## V7-37.2 Content authority and canonical concept

This section is normative.

Every governed content item traces to a canonical governed concept and to an authoritative source of truth in the frozen corpus. Content expresses that concept; it never becomes the source of truth. The model names, for each content class, which authority owns the concept and which function may author, review, and approve its expression, preserving the separation between the institutional meaning and its wording.

## V7-37.3 Approval and lifecycle

This section is normative.

The model defines a content lifecycle in which content moves from draft, through review and approval by the named authority, to an approved state, and eventually to superseded or retired. Each transition names who may perform it and what evidence it requires. Content is never silently changed: a change to approved content re-enters review, and superseded content is retained rather than overwritten. The model defines these governed transitions as definition only and performs none of them.

## V7-37.4 Localization governance

This section is normative.

Localization is governed as a first-class obligation, not an afterthought. Every approved English content item requires a French equivalent validated under chapter V7-36 before it may be considered complete, and the two are kept in step through their shared canonical concept so that neither language drifts. The model names how localization defects are recorded and how a change in one language triggers re-validation of the other.

## V7-37.5 Sensitivity and disclosure constraints

This section is normative.

Content governance honours the inherited privacy and disclosure postures. The model names which content classes may carry personal, financial, or restricted references and forbids content from disclosing more than the governed experience permits. No content governance activity expands access, and sensitive information is never embedded in content artifacts as sample or placeholder material.

## V7-37.6 Relationship to validation and handoff

This section is normative.

The content governance model is the definitional basis for the bilingual validation plan of chapter V7-36 and the content-readiness handoff of chapter V7-40. It records content-governance rules in register REG-702 as governed rules with an approval authority, a lifecycle rule, and a localization rule. Every rule is a definition; none is exercised, and none authorizes production content.

## V7-37.7 Explicit non-authorizations

This section is normative.

This chapter authorizes no implementation. It approves no content, publishes nothing, and validates no translation. It creates no production user interface, production content, validated translation, coded interface, design-system implementation, executable workflow, or interface or integration contract, and no procurement, sequencing, staffing, cost, pilot, rollout, or master development plan. Every controlled record remains in a not-implemented-or-not-proven posture.
