## V7-36.1 Purpose

This section is normative.

This chapter defines the bilingual semantic validation plan for the club-affiliation experience. It defines the plan only. No translation is validated and no bilingual equivalence is established by Package 4.

## V7-36.2 Objective and scope

This section is normative.

The objective is to confirm that the English and French expressions of every governed concept carry the same institutional meaning, the same user-facing consequence, and the same prohibited inference, so that neither language misleads a participant about status, obligation, evidence, or authority. The scope is the content terms and semantic requirements recorded in the frozen corpus, in which English and French trace to one canonical governed concept. Bilingual validation is a distinct family; it does not evaluate usability, accessibility, or visual finality.

## V7-36.3 Why machine and literal matching are insufficient

This section is normative.

The plan states that a machine translation and a literal word-for-word match are insufficient evidence of semantic equivalence. Governed meaning depends on institutional consequence, not surface wording: two literally faithful phrases can imply different obligations or authorities, and a fluent machine translation can silently alter what a status or notice means. Bilingual validation therefore requires qualified human judgement of institutional equivalence against the canonical concept, and the plan fails closed where only machine or literal evidence exists.

## V7-36.4 Method, qualification, and environment

This section is normative.

The plan names the validation method, the qualification required of validators fluent in both languages and literate in the governed domain, and the environment in which validation is conducted against the actual governed contexts. Validation covers not only static content but the meaning conveyed by status messages, error messages, notifications, and recovery guidance across the complete-state set, so that equivalence is confirmed in failure and recovery as well as in the default state.

## V7-36.5 Evidence, defect treatment, and prohibited inference

This section is normative.

Bilingual evidence is the qualified confirmation of institutional equivalence for each canonical concept. Defects are recorded as governed backlog items with owners and forward gates and re-evaluated under a named retest rule. The plan names its prohibited inferences: fluency is not equivalence, literal fidelity is not equivalence, and equivalence in one surface does not establish equivalence in another. No content is asserted to be validated, and no translation is asserted to be production content.

## V7-36.6 Acceptance authority and future gate

This section is normative.

The plan names the acceptance authority empowered to accept bilingual validation results and the forward gate at which acceptance may occur, distinct from the authors of the content. Package 4 defines who may accept bilingual evidence and when; it accepts none.

## V7-36.7 Explicit non-authorizations

This section is normative.

This chapter authorizes no implementation. It validates no translation, confirms no equivalence, and produces no production content. It creates no production user interface, production content, validated translation, coded interface, design-system implementation, executable workflow, or interface or integration contract, and no procurement, sequencing, staffing, cost, pilot, rollout, or master development plan. Every controlled record remains in a not-implemented-or-not-proven posture.
