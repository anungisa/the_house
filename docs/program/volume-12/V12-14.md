## Purpose

This chapter defines the functional, workflow, contract, integration, event,
webhook, callback, and provider evidence that would be required to accept the
club-affiliation vertical. Each requirement is recorded in REG-1202 under its
controlled kind. No command is executed, no interface is invoked, no event is
emitted, and no provider is engaged by this chapter.

## Functional and workflow evidence

Functional evidence would establish that the governed affiliation workflow behaves
correctly across its full behavioural surface, not merely on the intended path.
Functional evidence must cover the positive, negative, denied, stale, conflict,
duplicate, replay, interrupted, degraded, and recovery paths of every governed
transition. Evidence for only the intended path is, by definition, incomplete.

Two fail-closed distinctions govern the workflow surface:

- **Request timeout is not command failure.** A transport-level timeout observed by a
  caller does not establish that the governed command failed; the command may have
  succeeded, failed, or remained pending, and evidence must resolve which.
- **Submission receipt is not approval.** Acknowledging receipt of an affiliation
  submission does not establish that the submission was reviewed, that it was
  approved, or that any downstream state changed.

## Contract, integration, event, and webhook evidence

Contract evidence would establish that every governed interface — its commands,
queries, resources, events, webhooks, callbacks, providers, files, batches, and
migrations — conforms to its published contract. Integration evidence would establish
that the governed capability behaves correctly end-to-end across component and system
boundaries. Event-and-webhook evidence would establish that emitted events, delivered
webhooks, and received callbacks produce the governed business effect, exactly once
where required.

The fail-closed distinction is: **Event delivered is not business effect
reconciled.** Delivering an event, webhook, or callback is a transport outcome; it is
not proof that the intended business effect occurred and was reconciled against the
governed system of record.

## Provider evidence

Provider evidence would establish that provider-mediated capabilities — payment,
notification, and any externally operated dependency — behave correctly against the
real provider under governed conditions. Mocked or provider-simulated results are
useful for lower-level checks but do not establish integration or operation.

The fail-closed distinction is: **Mocked provider result is not provider integration
evidence is not operational proof.** A mocked provider outcome is not evidence that
the real provider integrates correctly, and provider integration evidence is not
proof of sustained operational behaviour.

## Prohibited inferences

Nothing here asserts that any command, query, interface, event, webhook, callback,
file, batch, migration, or provider integration exists or has been exercised.
Functional, contract, integration, event, webhook, callback, and provider evidence
are defined as obligations for a later, separately authorized package. No interface
is invoked, no provider is engaged, and no result is produced or accepted.
