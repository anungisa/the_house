## Purpose

This chapter defines the affiliation acceptance matrix, the twelve evidence
dossiers, and the executive release-decision brief that would govern acceptance of
the club-affiliation vertical. It defines the decision structure; it takes no
acceptance decision and authorizes no release.

## Affiliation acceptance matrix

The acceptance matrix records the acceptance classes required to accept the vertical:
implementation acceptance, technical acceptance, domain acceptance, security-and-
privacy acceptance, accessibility-and-bilingual acceptance, data-and-financial
acceptance, migration acceptance, operational acceptance, provider acceptance,
training-and-adoption acceptance, business acceptance, and the executive release
decision. Each acceptance class is recorded in REG-1201 and identifies its required
evidence, its acceptance authority, the reviewer qualification, the independence
requirement, the conditions it may attach, how dissent is treated, and its release
effect.

The acceptance ladder is fail-closed and its rungs are distinct:

- **Technical acceptance is not business acceptance.** Meeting technical criteria is
  not a business owner's acceptance of fitness for purpose.
- **Business acceptance is not operational acceptance.** Business acceptance is not a
  determination that the vertical can be operated and supported.
- **Operational acceptance is not executive release authorization.** Operational
  acceptance is not the executive decision to authorize release.

## Evidence dossiers

The twelve evidence dossiers are governed containers, each recorded in REG-1202 as an
`ACCEPTANCE_DOSSIER_REQUIREMENT` that identifies its authoritative contents and its
decision destination. A dossier names what evidence it must contain and which
acceptance authority or decision it feeds; it is neither evidence nor an acceptance.

## Executive release-decision brief

The executive release-decision brief is the governed input to the final release
decision. It aggregates the acceptance-class outcomes, outstanding conditions, open
material commitments, and release blockers, and routes them to the executive release
authority. The brief informs a decision; it does not make one, and it cannot supply
missing evidence.

The governing constraint is fail-closed: **Executive support is not evidence
substitution.** Executive endorsement, urgency, or sponsorship cannot substitute for
absent, failed, or unaccepted evidence; the release decision remains bounded by the
evidence.

## Prohibited inferences

Nothing here accepts any evidence, grants any acceptance class, or authorizes any
release. The acceptance matrix, the evidence dossiers, and the release-decision brief
are defined as governance obligations for a later, separately authorized package. No
acceptance decision is taken and no release is authorized.
