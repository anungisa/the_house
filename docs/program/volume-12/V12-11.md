## Purpose

This chapter opens Volume 12 Package 2. Package 1 defined, in documentary form, the
program-wide evidence classes, provenance requirements, gate criteria, acceptance
classes, and release-dossier structures. Package 2 applies that governance to a
single concrete vertical and defines the evidence that would be required to accept
it. **Club affiliation is the first evidence-definition vertical.** Package 2
answers one bounded question: what implementation, test, security, privacy,
accessibility, bilingual, financial, data, migration, operational, provider,
training, adoption, business, executive, and release evidence would be required to
accept the club-affiliation vertical — without creating, executing, submitting,
validating, accepting, or approving that evidence.

Package 2 defines obligations. It authorizes nothing. It manufactures no evidence,
runs no test, provisions no environment, executes no migration, engages no
provider, accepts no evidence, passes no acceptance or release gate, and releases
no service.

## The affiliation evidence domain

The club-affiliation vertical is decomposed into the affiliation evidence domain: a
governed catalogue of the capabilities whose acceptance evidence must be defined
before the vertical could ever be accepted. Each capability is recorded in REG-1201
as an `AFFILIATION_EVIDENCE_DOMAIN` record. For each capability the domain record
names its authoritative requirement, its institutional invariant, the actors it
affects, the responsibility split across the governed platform (the House), the
stakeholder experience layer (the Button), and external systems, the evidence
classes that would be required, the governed acceptance authority, the prohibited
inference, and the acceptance-dossier destination — its **governed documentary
disposition**. No capability may exist in the domain without an evidence destination
or a governed disposition.

The affiliation evidence domain covers, at minimum: club-affiliation application
intake; applicant identity and account establishment; membership and roster
context; organizational and jurisdictional scoping; authority, delegation, and
assignment; fee determination and financial settlement; provider-mediated payment
and notification; workflow state and lifecycle transition; document and record
capture; bilingual applicant experience; accessible applicant experience;
affiliation activation and standing; data-integrity and reconciliation;
migration of prior affiliation records; operational support and continuity; and
training, onboarding, and sustained adoption of the operating community.

## Acceptance-dossier architecture

Every capability's required evidence resolves to one of twelve acceptance dossiers
defined in Package 2. A dossier is a governed container that names its authoritative
contents and its decision destination; it is not itself evidence and not itself an
acceptance. The dossiers are indexed in REG-1202 as `ACCEPTANCE_DOSSIER_REQUIREMENT`
records and are projected, non-authoritatively, into the generated
affiliation-evidence-definition catalogue.

The acceptance-dossier architecture preserves three fail-closed distinctions that
govern the whole package:

- **Evidence-domain coverage complete is not evidence available is not evidence
  accepted.** A complete catalogue of required evidence proves only that the
  obligations are known; it proves nothing about whether any evidence exists or has
  been accepted.
- **Dossier section defined is not dossier populated is not acceptance granted.** A
  defined dossier structure is an obligation, not a filled dossier, and a filled
  dossier is not an acceptance decision.
- **Evidence requirement is not evidence availability is not evidence acceptance.**
  Defining what must be shown never asserts that it has been shown or accepted.

## Prohibited inferences

Nothing in this chapter or in the affiliation evidence domain may be read as
evidence that any capability is implemented, that any environment exists, that any
test has run, that any migration has executed, that any provider has been engaged,
that any evidence has been produced or accepted, that any acceptance or release gate
has passed, or that any release is authorized. The domain is a definition of
obligations that a later, separately authorized package would have to satisfy.
