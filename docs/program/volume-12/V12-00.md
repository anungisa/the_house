## Purpose

Volume 12 is the final evidence and decision-governance volume of the program. It
defines, in documentary form only, the evidence classes, provenance requirements,
sufficiency rules, decision rights, segregation-of-duty controls, gate criteria,
acceptance classes, material-commitment treatments, challenge procedures, and
release-dossier structures that must govern the program before any evidence can be
accepted or any release can be authorized.

This is Package 1 of Volume 12. It defines obligations. It authorizes nothing. It
does not manufacture evidence, run tests, approve implementation, accept evidence,
pass a final gate, or release a service.

## Inheritance

Volume 12 inherits, without modification, the frozen Volume 0 foundation and the
released Volume 1 through Volume 11 baselines. In particular, it builds directly on
the released Volume 11 operations, migration, adoption, and assurance governance
foundation, whose inherited release baseline is the annotated tag
`central-registration-volume-11-v1.0.0`. Volume 0 through Volume 11 remain frozen
and released; nothing in Volume 12 alters them.

## Volume 12 scope and authority

Volume 12 governs the final evidence and decision system. Package 1 defines:

- the evidence doctrine, hierarchy, sufficiency rules, and claim boundaries;
- the gate architecture, acceptance classes, decision rights, and segregation of
  duties;
- the evidence-object provenance, integrity, reproducibility, and retention model;
- the material-commitment, defect, exception, waiver, condition, and expiry model;
- the cross-domain evidence-intake and sufficiency model;
- the implementation, environment, configuration, deployment-path, and
  release-candidate evidence model;
- the business, operational, stakeholder, executive, and release-authorization
  model;
- the evidence deficiency, contradiction, challenge, revalidation, and revocation
  model;
- the final evidence-dossier, decision-brief, release-note, and handoff model;
- the deterministic Package 1 gate, release, and acceptance-governance assessment.

## Evidence-governance, acceptance, and release-decision authority

Three distinct authorities are defined and held apart. The evidence-governance
authority governs what counts as evidence, how it must be provenanced, and when it
is sufficient. The acceptance authority governs whether accepted evidence
discharges a defined obligation. The release-decision authority governs whether an
accepted, gated release candidate may be authorized for release. Evidence
submission is distinct from evidence acceptance, and neither confers a release.

## The controlling distinctions

The controlling principle of this volume is recorded as decision ADR-V12-001:

> Documentary approval is distinct from executed proof.

The following ladder of distinctions is fixed for the whole volume, and no stage
may be inferred from an earlier stage:

> Evidence requirement is distinct from evidence submitted.
> Evidence submitted is distinct from evidence valid.
> Evidence valid is distinct from evidence sufficient.
> Evidence sufficient is distinct from evidence accepted.
> Evidence accepted is distinct from gate passed.
> Gate passed is distinct from release authorized.
> Release authorized is distinct from deployment executed.
> Deployment completed is distinct from service stabilized.
> Service stabilized is distinct from institutional outcome accepted.

Authoring an evidence definition never constitutes, accepts, or implies evidence.
Defining an acceptance class is not accepting anything. Defining a release gate is
not passing it. Defining a release-authorization record is not authorizing a
release.

## Documentary effectiveness

A ratified Volume 12 record is documentary. It fixes an obligation. It is never a
source of evidence, an acceptance, or a release authorization, and evidence must
never be inferred from documentary completeness. A generated projection of this
corpus is never authoritative evidence.

## Decision segregation and independence

Evidence ownership, evidence review, evidence acceptance, and release authority are
segregated across distinct authorities with defined independence and
conflict-of-interest treatment. The volume preserves the separation of authoring,
closure and freeze, and pre-merge provenance binding for its own governance record.

## Non-authorizing posture

Every Volume 12 record carries six non-authorizing posture fields — implementation
status, operational status, execution status, evidence status, acceptance status,
and release status — fixed as:

```
authorizes_implementation: false
authorizes_operations: false
authorizes_release: false

implementation_status: NOT_IMPLEMENTED_OR_NOT_PROVEN
operational_status: NOT_OPERATIONAL_OR_NOT_PROVEN
execution_status: NOT_EXECUTED
evidence_status: NOT_AVAILABLE_OR_NOT_ACCEPTED
acceptance_status: NOT_ACCEPTED
release_status: NOT_AUTHORIZED
```

No record authorizes implementation, operations, or release.

## Amendment, freeze, and release-tag doctrine

Package 1 is closed by a closure record (V12-A), dispositioned by Gate V12-G1, and
frozen as PACKAGE-12-1. Provenance is bound after closure and again after merge
through post-merge amendments (V12-B and V12-B-1). Volume 12 is not tagged after

volume completion.

## Treatment of prior-volume requirements and unresolved obligations

All prior-volume requirements and unresolved obligations are inherited as governing
obligations. Volume 12 records the evidence and acceptance obligations that must be
discharged against them; it discharges none of them and infers no evidence from
their documentary completeness.
