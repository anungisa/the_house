## V6-I.4 Integrated protection conclusion and coverage

This section is normative.

The integrated baseline (V6-41) states one consolidated trust, protection, and
assurance definition for The House across authority, assets, actors, boundaries,
threats, rights, obligations, and controls. The consolidated catalogue (V6-42)
keeps assets under authority and classification, boundaries named by threats,
threats bound to affected assets or rights, and control objectives bound to
owners and future blocking gates. The identity and restricted-evidence synthesis
(V6-43) keeps identity, authentication, authorization, delegation, and privilege
distinct, retains full resource-aware authorization context, and applies
heightened controls to restricted evidence and privileged operations. The privacy,
records, disclosure, and provider-trust synthesis (V6-44) defines processing
purpose, minimization, notice, rights, disclosure authority, retention, and
records dependencies. The compliance, accessibility, bilingual, and accommodation
synthesis (V6-45) keeps financial and affiliation authorities segregated, defines
accessibility through user needs and complete task flows, requires English and
French governed meanings to remain equivalent, and preserves authority, privacy,
evidence, and equivalent outcomes through accommodation paths. The security
operations, incident, vulnerability, and notification synthesis (V6-46) keeps
events, incidents, breach determinations, and notifications distinct and defines
vulnerability, exception, expiry, remediation, and retest requirements. The
resilience, recovery, continuity, provider-exit, and assurance synthesis (V6-47)
keeps backup, restore, integrity verification, reconciliation, technical recovery,
and business acceptance distinct, keeps dependency failures failing closed with
institutional authority retained, and keeps metrics, exercises, operational proof,
self-attestation, and independent assurance distinct. Every posture is a
definition; none is a claim of implementation, verification, readiness, or proof.
