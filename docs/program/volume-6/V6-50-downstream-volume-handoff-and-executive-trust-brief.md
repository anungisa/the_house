## V6-50.1 Purpose and scope

This section is normative.

This chapter states the constraints Volume 6 transfers to the remaining volumes and
provides the executive trust brief for Volume 6 closure. It transfers constraints;
it introduces no new authority, no new control, and no new obligation, and it
authorizes no implementation.

## V6-50.2 Volume 7 handoff — experience and service design

This section is normative.

Volume 7 inherits the accessibility, bilingual-equivalence, inclusive-service,
notice, and rights constraints of this volume. Experience and service design must
preserve governed authority separations, must never let an experience layer own a
governed lifecycle decision, and must present governed workflows accessibly and
bilingually. Volume 7 introduces no governed lifecycle authority.

## V6-50.3 Volume 8 handoff — logical and physical design

This section is normative.

Volume 8 inherits the asset classification, trust-boundary, custody, isolation,
data-protection, and restricted-evidence constraints of this volume. Logical and
physical design must realize the defined trust boundaries and classifications and
must not weaken any fail-closed posture.

## V6-50.4 Volume 9 handoff — implementation and testing evidence

This section is normative.

Volume 9 inherits the control objectives, required-evidence statements, future test
classes, and House P0 protection matrix of this volume. Implementation and testing
must produce the defined implementation and test evidence, must verify behaviour
against the real data platform, and must not represent definition as proof.

## V6-50.5 Volume 10 handoff — operations and monitoring

This section is normative.

Volume 10 inherits the security-operations, monitoring, detection, incident,
resilience, backup, restore, recovery, and continuity definitions of this volume.
Operations must produce operational proof for the capabilities that require a real
environment and must preserve the separation of technical recovery from
business-acceptance.

## V6-50.6 Volume 11 handoff — assurance and independent validation

This section is normative.

Volume 11 inherits the assurance requirements, operational-proof dependencies, and
independent-assurance dependencies of this volume. Independent validation must be
performed by an independent party and must not be satisfied by self-attestation.

## V6-50.7 Volume 12 handoff — executive acceptance and material commitment

This section is normative.

Volume 12 inherits the material-commitment-pending items, procurement dependencies,
and executive material-commitment gate (EXEC-MCG) references of this volume.
Executive acceptance and material commitment — including provider selection,
procurement, staffing, cost, and delivery sequencing — are reserved to Volume 12
and are not made here.

## V6-50.8 Executive trust brief

This section is normative.

For the executive acceptance authority, Volume 6 closure affirms the following:

1. the complete security, privacy, compliance, accessibility, bilingual-equivalence,
   resilience, and assurance definition is consolidated and internally consistent;
2. every capability is traceable to a protected asset or right, an owner, and
   required evidence;
3. every capability is evidence-disciplined and marked not-implemented or
   not-proven;
4. governed authority separations are preserved throughout;
5. the House P0 protection matrix defines the minimum protection posture the
   platform must eventually evidence;
6. every unresolved item is owned, evidence-bound, and routed to a future
   destination;
7. no completed-gate reference remains active;
8. no implementation, procurement, provider selection, or material commitment is
   authorized;
9. the constraints transferred to Volumes 7 through 12 are stated; and
10. Volume 6 is disposed ready to govern the remaining volumes as a
    definition-complete baseline, not as an operational-readiness claim.

## V6-50.9 Explicit non-authorizations

This section is normative.

This chapter introduces no new authority, control, or obligation; authorizes no
implementation, procurement, provider selection, staffing, cost, or delivery
sequencing; makes no operational-readiness or assurance claim; and creates no
master development plan.
